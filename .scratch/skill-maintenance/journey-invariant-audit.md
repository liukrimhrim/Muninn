## G01. A known hint cannot be represented honestly when its time is unknown

**Finding type:** record-contract conflict. High confidence in the conflict between the cited declarations; no claim about a shipped defect or its frequency.

The [base record contract](compatibility-design.md#3-portable-logical-records) declares `Assistance = {shown_at: Time, text, criterion_ids, study?}`. Its `shown_at` is required and non-null. Assisted observations must validate against the attempt's scoped assistance. The [time-evidence addendum](../learning-observability/data-contract.md#3-occurrence-time-is-evidence-not-a-required-guess) correctly allows an answer to be saved with bounded or unknown occurrence time. However, its exhaustive allowed-field list covers session, attempt, study and review lifecycle fields; it does not cover nested assistance. That addendum changes the submission-time requirement explicitly, but does not change `Assistance.shown_at`.

**Counterexample.** An in-task hint was definitely displayed for criterion A. Its text and affected criterion are known, but a clock anomaly leaves no trustworthy display instant. The learner then submits a valid answer with unknown submission time. This is a permitted answer under the new time rules. The required hint entry has no honest value for `shown_at`: null violates its declaration; a guessed time, raw anomalous clock value or later receipt time invents an authoritative display time; omitting the entry discards known help and breaks the assisted-observation validator. The same problem exists when the hint time is bounded but not exact.

A separate exposure record can already carry uncertain timing. It does not, by itself, amend the requirement to preserve and validate this actual in-attempt assistance. This is narrower than the earlier suspected conflict about help on another screen: external exposure can legitimately be represented separately from a local hint flag.

**Decision needed.** Give nested assistance a representable timing-quality contract and state how the known help fact survives independently of its clock quality. Reconcile acceptance/import validation, scoped observations, reports and replay. Reuse the existing occurrence-time or exposure representation; a new timing service is not needed. A usable judgment with definitely known relevant in-task help must remain helped even if a dated claim is uncertain. Unknown time must neither erase the help nor convert the grade into an error.

**Baseline evidence:** compatibility lines 56, 67, 88 and 102; data-contract lines 60–70; tracking lines 78–82 and 93–97. These line numbers identify `a11431b`, not an assertion that later edits will keep the same numbering. Prior uncertain-time cases cover the parent attempt/study/review fields, not this nested hint record.

## G02. Frozen effort scope has no membership rule across curriculum revisions

**Finding type:** missing query decision. High confidence that the cited contracts do not choose the membership rule; they already express the intended full-cost accounting.

The [tracking default](../learning-observability/tracking-contract.md#2-a-concrete-default-plan) freezes the whole selected curriculum's effort scope for 21 days. The [effort records](observability-design.md#8-collection-and-reproducibility-contract) and [saved query](../learning-observability/data-contract.md#7-saved-report-payload-and-replay) express that scope as exact `Ref[]`; spans and sessions also point to exact enrollment revisions. Meanwhile, [ordinary enrollment changes](compatibility-design.md#6-curriculum-revisions-and-evidence-continuity) append revisions. Admission changes and completion checkpoints can revise an enrollment even when its learning goals stay the same.

**Counterexample.** Freeze tracking for enrollment E revision 1. Record 20 reliably placed minutes against E@1. A checkpoint appends E@2 with the same selected goals. A later session legitimately references E@2 and records 30 minutes of curriculum preparation/inspection, with no individual target attribution. Both spans are inside the effective cost interval, disjoint, and unambiguously concern the same curriculum. The intended whole-curriculum cost is **50 minutes**. A literal exact-reference join against the frozen E@1 includes only **20 minutes**. There is no declared rule telling the query to follow the enrollment identity or its eligible revision history instead. Following arbitrary reference links is also unsafe: a shared target can connect unrelated curriculum work.

This is not another temporal cutoff issue. Both spans are already before the selected cutoff, and neither has uncertain placement or disputed attribution. The latest `cost-cutoff-1` patch correctly controls *when* a span belongs. It does not specify *which evolving curriculum context* belongs to the frozen cost scope. The general rule for showing genuinely unallocated library overhead does not settle this clearly attributed E@2 work.

**Decision needed.** Define a versioned membership predicate for frozen cost scopes. Distinguish the stable curriculum/enrollment identity being followed from the exact revision used to freeze outcome targets and historical bindings. Specify which context links count, how later additions/removals affect effort membership, and how shared encounters are unioned once. New outcome targets must not silently enter the frozen outcome denominator. Persist the chosen scope semantics and included/excluded reasons so replay cannot substitute today's interpretation.

A reasonable direction is to follow the declared enrollment identity through permitted revisions during the fixed investment window, while retaining the original exact refs for provenance. If a comparison deliberately freezes narrower target work instead, name that different scope and display other curriculum overhead separately. The patch must choose explicitly; this audit does not silently approve either rule or add a new record service.

**Baseline evidence:** compatibility lines 20–40, 64–66 and 188; lifecycle lines 22 and 47; tracking lines 32, 50, 70 and 74; observability lines 87–91, 146 and 148; data-contract lines 208–221. Prior historical-cost cases vary dates and placement, but not the enrollment revision used by later spans.

## Suspicions rejected or kept outside the finding count

| Candidate | Why it is not another confirmed gap |
|---|---|
| External help versus `Observation.assisted` | The local flag can describe in-attempt assistance while exposure records qualify the full answer. The data contract retains both and tracking explicitly makes known available aids H. Reading the boolean alone would be a bad implementation, but the documents do not require that shortcut. W04 makes the integration obligation explicit. G01 is the stricter, demonstrable inability to encode a known local hint's uncertain time |
| Uncertain dates leaking into all raw ratios | Tracking already says to keep time-uncertain candidates separate, mark the as-of claim uncertain and avoid precisely dated plots. Raw grading coverage can describe stored submissions. An unconditional by-date percentage would violate those rules; the formula alone does not establish a separate design contradiction |
| Paused repair appearing in a later session | A paused repair encounter is distinct from pausing a target or enrollment. The former ends automatic encounter precedence; the latter stops offers. The reviewed text does not establish a promise that every repair pause lasts until explicit manual resumption. This wording merits UX qualification, not an invented blocker |
| Equivalent target revision while an owner is pending | Existing preservation of the same schedule identity, compatible-stream resolution and ownership rules give an intended invariant. A combined acceptance case is useful; no independent contradiction was established here |
| Late grading, corrected repair cycles or released owners | Existing chronology, cycle binding, correction and permanent-release rules already address these variants. They are not new findings merely because they require several contracts together |
| Missing baseline, an empty tracking window or a new exercise family | These intentionally withhold particular comparisons without generating extra measurement work. A missing result is not automatically a missing design decision |
| Real grading accuracy, accessible rendering, clock qualification and chosen adapter behavior | Already named implementation/semantic qualification dependencies; another document-level example cannot prove them |
| Causal learning gains, optimal review intervals, multi-user sync or selective deletion | Empirical questions or explicit first-release deferrals. No new scope or infrastructure is justified by this audit |

## Ownership, verification and stopping rule

The existing [data-contract investigation](../learning-observability/issues/09-data-contract.md) owns both drafting gaps. G01 touches the compatibility schema and assistance/exposure interpretation; G02 touches scope selection, report manifests and cost queries. The existing [everyday policy ticket](issues/14-everyday-learning-policy.md) still owns its previously open human choices. This audit does not reopen accepted web delivery, general learning scope, dashboard C or archive/whole-library deletion.

For this pass, stop after every listed journey has a documented disposition, every counted finding has an exact current source and a concrete counterexample, and rejected candidates are recorded. That stopping rule is now met. It establishes completion of this inspection, **not completeness of the system**. The subsequent [patch](journey-invariant-closure.md) resolves W01–W08 in the canonical contracts and records seven additional boundaries; its document-level closure remains distinct from complete-system or runtime qualification.

Verification consists of source-to-baseline byte comparison, direct re-reading of the conflicting declarations, local link/anchor and case-identity checks, and independent 20+30=50/shared-union arithmetic. These are documentary and arithmetic checks only. No production code, prototype, external service, real learner history or previously accepted policy was changed. Neither finding is ranked by measured severity or frequency.
