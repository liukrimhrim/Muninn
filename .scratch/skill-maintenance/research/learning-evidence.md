# Learning evidence for a relational practice system

Research date: 2026-10-12. Question: [What evidence supports relational practice and transfer between exercise formats?](../issues/02-learning-evidence.md). This is a research resolution, not evidence that the proposed system has improved anyone's learning. The supplied conversation is a source of hypotheses and preferences; its confident educational claims are not independent evidence.

The defensible design is to combine spaced retrieval with explicit practice of relationships and operational tasks, while preserving the distinction between what was observed and what was inferred. **Automatic reinforcement of prerequisite concepts or other exercise formats is not established by the evidence reviewed here.** A graph can describe content, generate useful contrasts and identify assessment gaps without asserting a law of human memory.

## Primary-source matrix

These are controlled studies or original modeling/evaluation papers, not a systematic review. A positive result applies to the studied comparison, materials and outcome. A nonsignificant difference does not establish equivalence.

| Primary source and location | What was actually studied and found | Boundary and design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| [Karpicke & Blunt, 2011, Science, pp. 772–775](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf), Experiments 1–2 | Retrieval of science-text content produced better week-delayed performance than elaborative concept mapping with the text available, including inference questions and a concept-map assessment. | This compares particular learning activities, not all possible graph interfaces. Retrieving a causal explanation or rebuilding a map from memory is a plausible exercise; merely displaying a graph is not the tested retrieval treatment. |
| [Chi et al., 1994, Cognitive Science, pp. 439–477](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chideleeuwchiulavancher_3.pdf), methods and posttest analyses | A small study prompted eighth graders to explain a circulatory-system text; the prompted group gained more than a read-twice control. It challenges guaranteed transfer, while the null result does not prove zero transfer. Domain and population differ from adult technical operations. |

## KARL: inspect the endpoint before importing its conclusion

The final [EMNLP paper, §7 and Table 4](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.784.pdf#page=8) reports 32 six-day study paths from 27 users. Each user used KARL and FSRS on separate sets of 20 trivia cards, reviewing ten cards daily for five days and taking a day-six test on all 20. The methods describe a within-subject comparison; they do not specify randomized or counterbalanced assignment of these sets.

Posttest accuracy was 0.86 for KARL and 0.88 for FSRS. Only all-card recall latency is marked significant in Table 4: 6.27 versus 6.82 seconds. Its efficiency measure divides the number correct by **mean posttest response time**, not total training time. The metric therefore cannot establish savings in cumulative learning effort. The article supplies no delayed unseen operational-task assessment.

Offline AUC/calibration gains concern prediction. The online result supports investigating a content-aware scheduler, not accepting automatic prerequisite strengthening or superiority in long-term operational competence. Similar observed accuracy is not a demonstrated noninferiority result. Repeated paths from some users also require care about the independent unit of analysis. These are limits on this inference, not evidence that the approach fails.

## Supported, plausible and still unproven

**Supported within the studied tasks:** retrieval can improve delayed retention and some inference/transfer outcomes; explanations and analogical comparison can help; spacing and feedback alter results; outcome format matters. These findings justify relational exercises and delayed assessment. They do not imply that every question must be difficult or every review must reconstruct a whole graph.

**Plausible design hypotheses:** a diagnostic scenario may exercise several components efficiently; relation prompts may reduce redundant fact review; a graph may improve selection of missing contrasts. Treat each as a prediction to test for this learner. A rich exercise has both a possible benefit and a time cost. “Touches five concepts” is not five measured learning gains.

**Unproven by this review:** a success on concept B strengthens every prerequisite A; transfer is symmetric; recognition success guarantees free recall; explaining a procedure guarantees execution; completing a task proves every component was necessary; a content embedding estimates the causal effect of practicing one item on another. No reviewed study establishes coefficients suitable for such automatic updates in this domain.

Separate three records: an observation (“completed this diagnosis unaided”), an inference (“may know the prerequisite”), and a learning intervention (“practiced this component”). A later independent probe can validate an inference. The initial inference should not masquerade as another direct review event. This is a conservative measurement decision, not a claim that transfer never occurs.

## A first one-learner Kubernetes-operations pilot

The proposed pilot asks: **Can this learner maintain delayed operational performance with a more useful allocation of a fixed practice budget?** It screens feasibility and personal value. It cannot establish population efficacy, calibrate a large transfer matrix, or isolate several changes made simultaneously.

1. **Freeze the target and evidence before practice.** Select a small set of genuinely useful diagnostic families, such as selecting the relevant workload, distinguishing a routing problem from an unhealthy workload, and checking the result of a remediation. A domain-competent reviewer must validate the scenarios, expected observations and outcome checks. Do not accept an LLM's plausible explanation as ground truth. Record prior familiarity using distinct baseline tasks; log outside work exposure throughout the pilot.

2. **Create matched, held-out variants.** For each family, author practice cases and unseen assessment cases before training. Vary names and layouts, but also vary symptoms, irrelevant clues and which diagnostic step resolves uncertainty. Include cases where the familiar fix is inappropriate. Label near transfer separately from a genuinely new combination of demands. A paraphrased known answer is a retention test, not convincing operational transfer.

3. **Compare one concrete alternative.** A reasonable baseline is direct item scheduling plus scheduled sandbox practice. A candidate uses relational/contrast exercises to replace selected review work while retaining sandbox practice. Fix the total learner-time allowance, feedback policy and resource-access policy. Randomize matched scenario families between conditions and alternate session order. This compares two practice packages; it does not identify a scheduler-only effect. Avoid a simple A-then-B crossover: learning does not wash out. Shared prerequisites can contaminate the comparison, so keep highly overlapping families together and report cross-condition exposure.

4. **Measure delayed performance, with assistance visible.** Propose assessments seven and 28 days after the last assigned practice, subject to the learner's actual objective. Use distinct task subsets at each delay and record all intervening exposures; an earlier test itself teaches and may affect later related tasks. Freeze documentation and hint access per outcome. A closed-book command recall probe and a realistic documentation-allowed terminal task answer different questions. Report both, without turning a lookup into an unaided success.

5. **Use observable outcomes.** The primary result is completion of an unseen task within a predeclared time limit, with correct final state and no prohibited action. Record diagnosis quality, verification, assistance, and unsafe or irrelevant actions separately. Keep correct completion time separate from timeout/failure. Freeze the rubric, preserve terminal traces and state checks, and have a reviewer blind to condition score ambiguous reasoning. A successful final state alone can conceal guessing, an accidental fix or an unnecessary destructive action.

6. **Count the whole cost.** Record active practice minutes, feedback time, sandbox setup/waiting, authoring/correction effort and recurring system-maintenance time separately. Also record completion/adherence and a short effort rating. Show delayed successes alongside actual minutes; do not hide lower accuracy inside a faster-answer ratio. One-time setup and recurring cost should remain distinguishable. Replacing ten cards with a twenty-minute lab is only an efficiency gain if the measured result justifies it.

7. **Set a decision rule rather than a weighted score.** Before seeing results, agree on the acceptable performance floor, what constitutes a material time saving and which errors force revision. First require the operational floor and coverage; then compare effort among alternatives that meet it. If results are mixed or sparse, retain direct probes and revise the exercise. Report item-level results and uncertainty; many attempts by one person are not many independent learners, and absence of a detected difference is not evidence of equivalence.

To test substitution specifically, add a later, narrow experiment: after comparable successful operational practice, randomly retain or omit a selected direct prerequisite review across matched families, then independently assess that prerequisite after a delay. Keep the rule otherwise identical and restore needed practice afterward. That tests the cost of omitting a review; the first bundle pilot does not. Until such observations accumulate, graph relations should guide **what to inspect next**, not silently erase review obligations.

The initial implementation therefore needs a curated exercise set, explicit evidence records, a time ledger and held-out assessments more urgently than a learned global transfer model. These are recommendations from the evidence and the proposed measurement design, not findings from a completed Kubernetes trial.
