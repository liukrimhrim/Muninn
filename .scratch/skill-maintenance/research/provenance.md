## What changed, and what remains open

The [design](../spec.md) now states the broader learning principles, distinguishes observations from learner estimates, describes adaptive granularity, and includes the binary-search example from the conversation. It treats the simple selection rule as a comparison baseline. It retains useful inference and compression as design questions rather than ruling them out through the FSRS API boundary.

The [representation decision](../issues/10-knowledge-representation.md), [inference decision](../issues/11-learner-inference.md), and [selection decision](../issues/12-adaptive-review-selection.md) hold the corresponding open questions. The existing [content-workflow decision](../issues/09-learning-workflow.md) now explicitly includes hybrid graph construction and links to existing knowledge. No human-dependent decision was closed by this audit.

The existing spike compares blind graph credit with explicit evidence. It cannot decide whether calibrated indirect inference or adaptive review compression works. Its deliberately weak comparator supplies a counterexample, not a full comparison of useful designs. No new prototype, literature investigation, learner experiment or calibrated inference model was completed in this correction.

The original weighted-sum formulas remain excluded because the active user instructions prohibit weighted sums. Their underlying distinctions—learning benefit, diagnostic value, overlap and cost—remain part of the design. The coverage problem can be investigated using explicit constraints and comparisons rather than a combined arbitrary score.
