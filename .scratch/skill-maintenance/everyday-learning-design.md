# From learning evidence to the next useful action

Status: first-release behavior and operating defaults finalized in [Design Baseline 1](design-baseline.md), 2026-10-16. The user requested finalization after the design and review passes. These are revisable design choices, not empirical optima or authorization to build. The existing policy investigation retains later validation and extension questions.

The core learning sequence remains: try an appropriate larger exercise → locate a supported gap when possible → give useful help → check its use in a fresh task → check later. A direct fact can need a direct recall question instead. Preserve the user's general scope, web delivery, analytical dashboard C, corrections, and progress through architecture changes.

## 1. Decisions this proposal makes explicit

The [lifecycle contract](learning-lifecycle-contract.md) supplies the subsequent cross-contract patch: enrollment admission, authoring intents and accepted teaching, ordinary offer ownership, occurrence-ordered replay, and prospective timing recovery. The [tracking contract](../learning-observability/tracking-contract.md) supplies plan creation and claim-qualified aggregation. Use those named extensions together with the unchanged selection/repair policies below.

The follow-up [four-gap closure](integrated-design-closure.md) makes descendant correction outcomes, recoverable caught-up indicators, shared-content exposure and historical cost boundaries explicit. Use its linked canonical rules; a replaced historical failure is not a current obligation, and a correction does not erase a valid later demonstration.

| Problem in the existing draft | Proposed rule | Reason and alternative |
|---|---|---|
| A dated mistake can be mistaken for present mastery | Show last observed performance, review timing and unresolved concerns separately | A fitted decay score would imply knowledge the first release cannot estimate |
| Recovered units can remain in a weakest list forever | Separate unresolved difficulty from immediate recovery awaiting a later check | Do not manufacture additional remediation to occupy five positions |
| “Fresh” can mean a renamed copy | State what changed, what capability stayed fixed, and what prior answer exposure is relevant | Arbitrary question variety can change the assessed skill instead of testing it again |
| Small authored selections do not define daily priorities | Continue a current encounter briefly, then order ready pending work by waiting time; introduce new work afterward unless the learner chooses otherwise | No weighted utility score or automatic quota is justified |
| Diagnosis limit is per attempt in one document and per session in another | At most three diagnostic prompts per session, also at most three on the same unresolved concern across sessions | A restart must not reopen an unlimited interrogation; three is a provisional burden limit |
| “Change the explanation” leaves repeated repair undefined | After one failed repair, change the teaching approach; after two supported unsuccessful cycles, stop automatic repair and review the content/assessment/target | Two is a provisional product safeguard, not a learning law |
| Dashboard inspection can contaminate the next check | Keep answer-bearing evidence collapsed; offer checking before revealing it, and preserve any actual exposure | Reading an answer does not count as a retrieval or a failure |

All numeric limits above are versioned starting policies, not research-derived optima. No existing learner preference is re-opened. These defaults can be reviewed using the [worked cases](everyday-learning-cases.md), without asking the learner to approve field names.

## 2. What the evidence says, and how old it is

### Three separate questions

For each exact target/requirement/mode, show:

1. **What happened?** The most recent usable observation and its date: correct without task-specific help, supported error, helped answer, or unresolved evidence. Keep the exact question, response, interpretation and applicable content version reachable.
2. **What is still needed?** An initial check, clarification, focused help, immediate verification, a later review, or content/assessment review. This is an action need, not a mastery score.
3. **When should we check again?** Use the recorded schedule: not yet due, due now, overdue, or timing unknown. “Overdue” describes the plan; it does not mean forgotten.

An old correct answer remains an old correct answer. A recent mistake does not invalidate successful observations in other modes or units. Passage of time alone creates no error event. A changed target definition preserves earlier success under its original meaning; the new definition may need new evidence. Do not average recall, explanation and execution into one score.

The UI can say “Last correct without help: 12 September. Review overdue since 19 September.” It must not say “Mastery fell to 34%” without a separately qualified prediction model. If the clock or exposure timing is uncertain, say what is unknown rather than inventing recency. Use exact dates; avoid an undocumented “recent enough” threshold.

### Baseline shortlist: `observed-concerns-2`

Keep the existing C placement and evidence drilldown. This is the baseline successor to the prototype's `observed-review-order-1`. The prototype has not been updated to this rule. The user approved its presentation direction and subsequently requested finalization of the revised design; policy quality still needs real qualification.

- The primary list contains up to five units with an **unresolved, accepted, independently supported error**, localized to that unit and requirement. Label it “Knowledge units to revisit” with the explanation “Based on recorded difficulty; this is not a measurement of everything you know.”
- Order these units by the length of the current consecutive error run within a comparable stream, descending. Count distinct exercise episodes, not criteria, corrections, duplicate deliveries or several facets of one answer. Equal evidence is tied. Use stable identity only to render a deterministic order, and disclose omitted tied units.
- A missing, helped or unresolved scheduled check interrupts a consecutive run; it does not clear a previous concern. Show “last supported error on [date]; latest check unresolved” and route to resolving/checking the evidence before choosing remediation. Do not silently bridge unknown observations into an error streak.
- A later qualifying independent success that actually covers the failed requirement changes the unit to **recovery observed**. Remove it from the primary error list. If it follows repair in the same session, show it in an adjacent “Check again later” group until the ordinary delayed review is satisfied. Do not invent an extra review just to clear a flag.
- A later due check with qualifying independent success closes the recovery concern. A new supported error later opens a new concern linked to the old one, without treating every old error as a continuous streak. If no teaching occurred, a later qualifying due success can directly close the error concern.
- Units with no adequate check, uncertain timing, incompatible revisions, or grading disputes appear in **“Need better evidence”**, separately from demonstrated difficulty. A unit can have an error in one mode and unknown evidence in another; show both and offer actions for the named mode.
- Group by stable knowledge identity and show every affected requirement/mode inside it. Rank a unit by its highest-priority eligible stream, without counting that unit five times. Whole-task failure that cannot be localized goes into “Needs diagnosis”; it is not an error assigned to every constituent unit.
- An accepted misread correction removes the corresponding error eligibility. It does not automatically create success. A grading correction changes the effective historical interpretation and can close, reopen or supersede a concern; it never creates a new learning episode.

Age and due state remain visible alongside this order. They are not secretly weighted into it. The list answers “where have checks found unresolved difficulty?” The daily selector answers “what should fit into this session?” Those questions can have different orders. Choosing practice from the list is an explicit learner priority, not evidence that this ranking maximizes learning.

The selected dashboard date and saved report cutoff apply to all these calculations. Never pull today's resolved concern into last month's report. A report using version 1 retains that rule and its old ranking; version 2 is a different recorded definition. The primary chart's fixed target denominator and raw S/E/H/U/M/N statuses do not change because the shortlist changes. A claim-qualified chart additionally uses the separately versioned QS/QE/QH/QU/QM/QX partition; it does not reuse raw success counts for delay/exposure claims.

## 3. Exercise families, freshness and comparability

### Different claims need different variations

| Relationship to an earlier exercise | Example | What a successful response may support |
|---|---|---|
| Same cue, same fact | Ask “What does mavo mean?” again after a delay | Legitimate recall of that exact association; novelty is not required |
| Surface variation | Rename Mira to Noor in an otherwise identical search explanation | Performance on a similar prompt; no additional transfer claim |
| New instance under the same target | Change list sizes and ask for the *increase* in reductions, or give a new boundary case while preserving the required principle | Another instance of the same capability if its rubric and demands genuinely match |
| Changed context or combination | Ask the learner to select a search method when sortedness is not guaranteed | A changed/extended target or explicit transfer check; do not splice it into the old trend without an accepted comparability mapping |
| Changed mode | Recognize the correct command instead of recalling it, or describe a procedure instead of executing it | Evidence for that mode only |

For integrated or procedural repair, the recommended verification is a new instance that forces the corrected decision or connection to be used. It must not be solvable just by copying the prior final answer. Reusing the method just taught is desirable: changing halving from 65,536→1,024 to 32,768→2,048 changes the required count from six to four while preserving the method. Numeric substitution can therefore be meaningful; merely changing names while the old substantive answer remains sufficient is surface variation. Check the mapped action rather than counting changed words. Conversely, do not force novelty that changes a simple fact-recall goal.

### Authoring and acceptance gate

Before a candidate can be served, preserve this small review packet:

- Exact source/target/requirement revisions, prompt, allowed aids, rubric and acceptable alternative responses.
- Family identity and a plain statement of the solution demands: what decisions, relationships or recalled content the learner must supply.
- Relationship to the previous/practice examples: same cue, surface change, new instance, or changed demand. State the changed features and the features held fixed.
- Required background, irrelevant complexity and answer-bearing cues. For a new instance, include a short explanation of why the old final answer alone cannot satisfy it.
- Criterion-by-criterion coverage, including criteria intentionally left unobserved. A solution can legitimately bypass a presumed prerequisite; do not credit that prerequisite unless the response supplies its evidence.
- Expected setup, answer and feedback cost, its origin and uncertainty. Estimates supplied by an author/model remain estimates.

Acceptance has two layers. Deterministic checks reject unresolved refs, missing rubric bindings, stale definitions, invalid answer anchors and an absent required condition. Semantic review checks source support, solution correctness, sufficient information, valid alternatives, actual elicitation of the capability, cue leakage and unintended difficulty. A schema-valid model reply is not semantic approval. Record who/what checked it and their protocol; a second model is not an independent human reference.

For assisted personal material, the system may provisionally accept a candidate under the versioned authoring protocol after the listed checks; label its provenance and allow immediate dispute. This does not claim expert certification. Known consequential disagreement holds that candidate. Try one targeted regeneration with the failed checks; after a second rejected candidate, leave an authoring need, offer explanation or another ready target, and preserve the learner's time. Do not generate indefinitely until something merely passes the schema.

Curated examples should have an independently checked reference before they are used to qualify the system. For new generated material, first-release qualification inspects unseen outputs and records substantive defects and correction effort. Content acceptance is always revisable; it is not learner mastery.

### Comparable does not mean equal difficulty

Use a contract match for ordinary descriptive comparison: same target action, mode, conditions, required reasoning and rubric meaning, with no known material change in background demands. A different instance can meet that contract without a calibrated difficulty claim. Show the family/context alongside a trend. If a candidate adds a new theorem, unfamiliar vocabulary, a new tool or more complex reasoning, use another stratum/target or an explicit accepted mapping. Withhold the pooled progress claim until comparability is justified.

Do not numerically adjust scores for guessed difficulty. A short-answer success and multiple-choice success are not exchangeable because their text is similar. Keep original observations even when a later audit determines that a question was easier, ambiguous or invalid; revise the interpretation/mapping and annotate the affected chart.

### Exposure, no-help answers and delay

Eligibility is evaluated against the bound answer's production/submission interval, not the later grading or rating arrival time. Feedback delivered after submission cannot retroactively become help on that response, although it remains relevant to future checks. A revealed answer or explanation still available during the response must be closed or treated as available assistance under that target's conditions. A late report of exposure that truly preceded the answer can revise its qualification with provenance; it does not rewrite old saved reports. The [exposure-recovery contract](learning-lifecycle-contract.md#5-exposure-has-an-end-and-uncertainty-has-a-prospective-recovery) now defines ongoing/ended availability, supported end bounds and a prospective checkpoint for unknown historical exposure. It preserves unknown past timing while allowing future eligible checks.

“Unseen prompt,” “no task-specific help during this attempt,” and “after a qualifying delay” are separate properties. Exposure to an answer, explanation or relevant worked example can affect interpretation even if the learner subsequently answers without an in-task hint. Preserve the actual answer and exposure; do not invent a failure or erase a success.

Opening the answer-bearing part of the dashboard records exposure at its actual scope. Opening a collapsed unit title or a result count does not. The UI offers **Check before seeing the answer** while it can still do so honestly. If the answer was already revealed, offer **Practice now**; label the result immediate practice and retain the scheduled later obligation. A superficially different prompt cannot undo the exposure.

For the simple capability policy, recording relevant answer-bearing exposure also records the check's eligibility deferral, even if the learner closes the page without answering. An intended due check before that deferral expires cannot trigger ordinary seven-day advancement, regardless of session ID. Record any observed result, keep the old obligation, and propose another check in a later session. The proposed operational next-offer time is the later of the existing due time and 24 hours after a trustworthy last relevant exposure; this is a conservative starting policy consistent with the existing 24-hour post-help check, not an estimated forgetting curve or a guarantee of independent retention. Preserve original due time separately from next-offer time; an exposure must not bring a future scheduled check forward. If exposure time is bounded, use its upper bound; if unknown, withhold automatic delay certification and offer ordinary practice or a supported prospective condition checkpoint. Once the relevant exposure is known to have ended, a future check can become eligible after the declared wait without rewriting the unknown history. Do not require the learner to fabricate a time. Normal target-relevant content is scoped: viewing one unrelated unit must not defer the whole curriculum.

Exposure need not have occurred in the app: a learner-reported answer view or relevant outside study is retained with its time quality. Never promise the app knows all exposure. A later check can satisfy the simple scheduling policy yet fail a research cohort's longer exposure-free window. Endpoint eligibility and scheduler transitions remain separate, and no later retry replaces the originally assigned endpoint.

For direct FSRS recall, only an actual eligible recall attempt with the learner's actual rating can update its stream. An answer view creates no review. An attempt deliberately served as immediate practice after revealing the answer is not silently converted into a scheduled review. The rating/eligibility adapter must expose the distinction, not reconstruct it from whether the answer text was correct.

Resolve relevant exposure across targets with [exposure-scope-1](learning-lifecycle-contract.md#shared-content-across-different-targets), including opposite retrieval directions and revealed procedure steps. Shared content can affect conditions without awarding shared achievement. A missing candidate mapping is uncertain, not absence; old occurrences discovered for a new target keep their original time. No arbitrary graph-wide deferral is implied.

### Held-out checks

The normal practice generator receives the target, constraints, prior relevant exposures and required changes, not a hidden evaluation answer. Preauthored assessment variants and their solutions stay out of practice/teaching prompts. An evaluation candidate gets its designation and endpoint assignment before the answer. If it is revealed or accidentally used for practice, mark that planned endpoint contaminated/unavailable as appropriate; do not replace it retrospectively with the learner's best retry. A new future assignment follows a new prospective plan.

## 4. Planning a session within real time

### Start and resume

Use the [admission lifecycle](learning-lifecycle-contract.md#1-selecting-goals-does-not-create-a-review-backlog): selected/unstarted goals are distinct from pending admitted work. The session preview admits only chosen new targets. Shared exact targets retain one schedule, and pausing an enrollment gates its offers without erasing evidence or stopping another active enrollment.

Start with a purpose and time budget, using the current curriculum context by default. From a library view, the learner can select several active curricula in a visible order. Save that scope; paused/archived curricula are excluded unless explicitly resumed. Shared exact targets have one schedule and one need even if they belong to several selected curricula.

Show a short proposal: “10 minutes. Start with one overdue check. Two other checks may fit. New material remains available.” The learner can choose another target, start new material, change the budget, or stop. These are navigation choices, not permission dialogs. Record overrides and their effect; do not mark skipped work as completed.

Resume an unfinished encounter before generating a replacement. Preserve its original prompt, partial answer, help and remaining concern. Reloading the page does not reset time already recorded or diagnostic limits. If the source contract changed, preserve the draft, explain why it cannot continue, and offer compatible work instead.

### Selection order: `bounded-session-1`

First remove candidates whose content, assessment, scope or delay conditions do not permit the requested action. Keep their unmet needs visible with a reason. Then:

1. **Finish the current short encounter if useful and affordable.** A clarification supported by the just-given answer, focused help for its supported gap, or immediate verification can precede older work, within the limits in section 5. This precedence ends at the selected concern's bounded repair/verification sequence, resolution, review or pause. If verification reveals a different gap, save it in ordinary waiting order rather than extending the encounter indefinitely. Resuming a real unfinished draft preserves that draft, but an old chain of concerns does not inherit automatic priority in every new session.
2. **Take ready pending work.** Combine due/overdue checks, paused supported repairs and unresolved checks that can now be usefully resolved. Sort by `waiting_since`: the original due timestamp for a due obligation, otherwise when the supported need first arose. A repair created from a failed due check retains that due obligation's waiting time. Corrections, reopens and duplicate deliveries do not reset it. If historical occurrence is unknown, use a separately labelled, trustworthy `first_recorded_ready_at` as the selection anchor; it records when the app first knew the need was ready, not when the learner learned or failed. Preserve this anchor so recurring newer dated work cannot permanently outrank it. If even current reliable time is unavailable, use the saved ready-registration order as a disclosed fallback for all affected needs; never certify elapsed learning delay from that order.
3. **Introduce new material when no ready pending action fits**, or when the learner explicitly chooses it. Show the existence of remaining reviews and that new material will add future checks. Do not invent a percentage quota or estimate a sustainable learning rate from no data.

Within the same waiting time, use the selected curriculum order, then its existing goal order, then stable target identity. Shared targets take their earliest position. A candidate may cover lower-priority needs as well as the first one; it must not omit the first feasible need merely to maximize a count of easy targets. When all selected needs fit, prefer lower full cost, then fewer exercises, then stable exercise identity. For partial coverage, compare covered needs lexicographically in the explicit priority order. No weighted sum combines urgency, mistakes and time.

If the oldest need has no suitable exercise or cannot fit, offer the next affordable action and state what was left. Keep its original waiting time. At session end, show repeatedly unserved needs and the reason: too long for this budget, content held, uncertain assessment, or explicitly deferred. Offer a longer session, narrower valid exercise, or pause of the curriculum. Do not secretly replace a required execution with a short verbal description to clear the queue. A small budget cannot promise fairness to tasks that never fit.

### Cost and feasibility

Include setup, responding, feedback and blocking waiting. For diagnosis, reserve the cost of the diagnostic question plus the largest applicable follow-up branch, including corrective help and verification where that branch requires them. A diagnostic question with two branches costing 90 and 150 seconds needs room for the larger branch, not their average. This is a feasibility check, not a prediction that the learner will behave as authored.

Use an explicit estimated range when available; fit against its upper bound. Do not turn an unknown cost into zero or a spurious exact value. Unknown-cost work can be offered as one clearly labelled exploratory action when the learner chooses it, with stopping available; the app cannot claim a full plan fits. Use measured history only within comparable formats/conditions, show sample count and variability, and keep authoring estimates as fallback. No fitted cost model is required initially.

After each actual action, recompute remaining time using the existing timing-quality contract. Unknown time does not become a saving. If the budget is exceeded, preserve the answer and feedback; stop offering more work by default. Never discard a response at a countdown boundary. Explicit continuation changes the budget; it does not rewrite actual elapsed effort.

### Returning after an absence

Show due needs once per exact target, not one task for each missed calendar reminder. Preserve the original due timestamps and distinguish missed measurement endpoints from pending practice. Do not reset all progress, award completions, mark every target failed or create a pile of identical overdue questions. Use the same bounded selector, starting with suitable older needs. One combined exercise can serve several pending targets only through its actual evidence.

Unattempted or unresolved targets remain pending. A paused curriculum retains history and due needs but stops contributing to the active session; shared active targets continue. New-material preference can override the default without pretending the backlog disappeared. Long absence alone never triggers an automatic whole-curriculum diagnostic exam.

## 5. Diagnosis and repair that can stop

### A concern has continuity

A concern is a saved, scoped reason to inspect or help: an accepted local error, a whole-task ambiguity, or a disputed assessment. It points to the actual evidence and exact target requirements when known. It is not a diagnosis merely because it has an ID. A new session or differently worded question does not create a new concern to evade its history.

| Evidence/state | Next useful action | What must not happen |
|---|---|---|
| Sound reasoning and a supported trivial misread | Explain the mismatch briefly; accept the corrected interpretation | Re-teach the topic or demand needless arithmetic |
| Omitted detail with no contrary claim | Ask a neutral question only if its answer changes the next action | Record inability as fact |
| Local supported error, useful teaching already clear | Offer focused explanation/worked step | Require three diagnostic questions first |
| Two plausible causes imply different help | Ask one discriminating question with explicit branches | Walk every prerequisite mechanically |
| Answer reveals a bad question, source or rubric | Hold the affected content/assessment and route to correction | Punish the learner or label the fault a knowledge gap |
| Question/assessment remains ambiguous | Retain uncertainty; offer worked example under uncertainty, review or pause | Invent an exact missing unit |
| Independent success after focused help | Mark immediate recovery and preserve later review | Declare durable mastery |
| Fresh verification shows the same supported error again | Change teaching once, then review if another cycle fails | Repeat paraphrases indefinitely |

### Diagnostic budget

Use **three diagnostic prompts per persisted session**, across all concerns, and **three per unresolved concern across sessions**. Clarifications and slip checks count. A displayed diagnostic prompt counts even if unanswered; resuming the same bound draft does not count twice. Hints/explanations are study, not diagnostic prompts, but their time and exposure still count. A compound question with three independent diagnostic requests uses three slots; wrapping questions together must not bypass the limit.

Stop earlier when the answer already identifies useful help, the question cannot discriminate the relevant alternatives, or the remaining budget cannot fund its follow-up. Exhaustion changes the route to support under uncertainty, assessment/content review or pause. It creates no failure grade. The learner can choose to continue talking, but that is a visible manual continuation, not an automatically replenished diagnostic budget or evidence that the uncertainty is resolved.

New accepted evidence can make a different concern necessary, but it must name what changed and link the previous concern. Merely reopening the same unresolved issue or asking the same question differently cannot reset its count. Corrections preserve the burden actually incurred even if the original concern is later invalidated.

### Repair cycles

A repair cycle consists of completed focused teaching and a subsequent eligible check of the same scoped requirement. Help display without completion, an unanswered prompt, or unjudgeable evidence is an incomplete cycle, not an unsuccessful one. A check with relevant in-task help remains assisted and cannot establish independent repair. All work still contributes to effort.

After the first cycle with another supported error, the next automatic proposal must change the teaching approach and state how: use a worked example instead of an abstract rule, contrast a valid case with a near miss, or revisit the missing connection. Do not automatically split a knowledge unit or weaken its target. Semantic content changes follow the existing revision/review rules.

After **two completed cycles with supported unsuccessful verification** on the same concern, stop automatic remediation. Present: “These two approaches have not resolved this yet. Review the explanation or assessment, revisit a prerequisite, or pause.” Check content correctness and task alignment before proposing a learner prerequisite gap. New prerequisite practice requires evidence or a plainly labelled diagnostic hypothesis; the graph alone is insufficient.

If a verification is unanswered, unjudgeable or assisted, do not wait for two error cycles forever. After one unanswered/unjudgeable verification, offer review/pause or one justified clarification within the diagnostic/time limits. After an assisted answer, retain the independent-check need and offer a later check or pause; do not repeat teaching solely because the learner used a hint. Do not launch another automatic repair cycle until new evidence supports it. The user can explicitly choose further practice; record it while retaining the unresolved concern. Persistent difficulty does not justify an escalating compulsory workload.

The count survives session boundaries. It stops governing when an accepted independent verification resolves that concern or a correction supersedes its basis. A later recurrence is linked but distinct. A due review need can remain after immediate recovery. No universal “failed learner,” ability score or permanent remedial track is created.

## 6. From dashboard to practice and back

### The visible workflow

| Step | What the learner sees | Default next action and alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Choose what to work on** | Unit, mode, dated evidence, why flagged, due state; answer text initially collapsed | “Work on this”; “See the evidence” |
| **2. Understand the next action** | One sentence: “Check whether this is still difficult,” “Practice this connection,” or “Review an uncertain judgment”; estimated full effort | “Start”; “Change task”; “Back” |
| **3. Do the learning action** | One clear task, response area, help and stop; a visible step indicator | Submit answer or request help; neither role nor expectation is hidden |
| **4. See what the answer established** | Supported criteria, uncertainty, scoped help, and a short explanation | Targeted help/verification if justified; “Question this judgment”; “Finish for now” |
| **5. Return to progress** | What changed, what did not, time used, and the next later check | Return to the same dashboard scope/date/filter, with current changes clearly marked |

The action is derived from current evidence, not just the card's rank. An older error with intervening unknown evidence normally gets a neutral check, not presumed remedial teaching. A supported unresolved error can get focused help. A recovered unit gets its due check when eligible; no extra practice is demanded solely because it was once in the shortlist. An assessment dispute leads to review before learner remediation.

### Inspection can teach

“See the evidence” first shows dates, mode, result and a reason reviewed as non-answer-bearing for this target, such as “The response did not establish the required relationship.” A paraphrase or explanation can reveal the answer without quoting it; “the singular subject controls the verb” belongs behind the reveal when that relationship is tested. Revealing the original response, solution, relevant diagnostic hint or worked explanation records scoped exposure. If a summary's safety is uncertain, collapse it too. The app then offers practice under those conditions; it does not call the next response an untouched delayed test. No repeated warning dialog is needed: use a short inline statement, “You just viewed the answer; a check now will count as practice.” Merely entering the dashboard is not automatically content exposure or completed study.

### Corrections

“Question this judgment” offers ordinary language reasons: “I misread it,” “My answer is valid,” “The question is unclear,” and free text. Preserve the response and the learner's explanation. A self-report opens review; it is not an automatic success. Accept a correction when its evidence supports it, otherwise retain the uncertainty without making the learner defend themselves through endless probes.

Before acceptance, show the concrete effect of the proposed correction. A misread interpretation without sufficient demonstration can say “This removes the error flag; it does not establish a correct check.” If review finds that the original answer already met all required criteria, say “This changes the earlier judgment to correct; no new attempt is added.” Historical E→S is permitted when supported; it is a corrected observation, not new learning. An accepted revision updates affected current conclusions and schedules once, at the original event time. Old saved reports stay frozen. Corrections to source/target meaning follow content versioning rather than pretending the old question asked the new one.

### Historical report versus current action

A historical report stays a historical report. “Work on this now” re-resolves the unit against current content, accepted assessments, exposure and schedule before selecting an exercise. If the concern is already resolved, explain that and offer current practice; do not replay stale remediation. If its target changed or was archived, show the current mapping/status and compatible options. Do not silently switch scope or revive an archived curriculum.

Save the return destination, selected date, filters and focused unit. Returning to a historical view keeps it frozen and shows a link to the new current result. Returning to a live view refreshes it and annotates the new attempt or correction. Keyboard focus returns to the initiating unit/action; a removed flag has an announced replacement/status message. Chart data and action reasons must also be available as text.

### Interruption and unavailable services

Save a draft before assessment. Model timeout leaves “Answer saved; assessment pending,” without a grade or learning-state update. Stop or close preserves the same prompt/answer, exposure and concern. Restore/reopen resolves the next action from current saved state before showing it. Content or assessment service failure does not prevent inspecting local history or using already accepted exercises. Apply the existing one-retry rule; do not hide repeated calls in the time ledger.

## 7. Record changes needed for this behavior

Reuse the [compatibility records](compatibility-design.md) and [observability addendum](../learning-observability/data-contract.md). This section extends their unreleased proposal; it does not introduce a service, queue backend or migration framework. All new refs obey their existing closure, revision, receipt and restore rules.

| Existing boundary | Proposed addition | Why it is necessary |
|---|---|---|
| `exercise` | A versioned `design_review` containing family demands, variation kind/basis, changed/held-fixed features, contract-match rationale, required background, cue checks, review actor/protocol and disposition | “Fresh and comparable” must be inspectable rather than a model adjective |
| `exercise.expected_seconds` / `selection.candidates` | Retain existing component estimates; add their origin, optional lower/upper total estimate, and unknown/estimated/measured status | Unknown cost must not masquerade as a fit or saving |
| `session` | Replace singular `enrollment` with ordered `enrollments: Ref[]` in this still-unreleased contract; capture chosen scope and budget revisions | A library session can span curricula without double-counting shared work |
| `attempt` | Optional `concern: Ref`, presentation reservation/acknowledgement, `verification_of` binding, and bound exercise/exposure eligibility decision with rule/basis/reasons | Persist diagnostic burden and the exact repair cycle; distinguish prompt novelty, no-help answer and delayed qualification |
| `study` | Optional `concern: Ref`; keep actual started/completed status and existing scoped targets | Connect help to its later verification without inventing completion |
| New `concern` record | Origin attempt, exact target/requirement scope or explicit unlocalized scope, linked prior concern, status/route, effective evidence, resolution reason and optional next-offer time | Keep repeated diagnosis/repair continuous across sessions and revisions |
| `selection` | Original waiting-since basis, preserved first-recorded-ready anchor/order when occurrence is unknown, current ready/blocked reason, explicit override and cost/branch basis | Explain why a task was chosen or left behind without recomputing history under a new policy |
| `schedule` | Keep original `due_at`; next-offer deferral is separate and cites exposure/learner choice | A postponed presentation is not forgotten knowledge, completed work or a new due success |
| Report definition | `observed-concerns-2` rule and inclusion/closure/streak rules; underlying episode refs | Changing flags must not silently reinterpret saved version-1 reports |

`concern` state is a saved decision record, not independent learning evidence. Proposed routes are diagnosis, repair, verification, later_check, assessment_review and content_review; status is open, paused, resolved or superseded. `scope` contains exact `{target: Ref, requirement_ids: text[]}` entries or an explicit unlocalized origin attempt. `basis: Ref[]` cites accepted assessments/corrections; `previous_concern: Ref?` links recurrence or changed scope. `resolution_reason` and `next_offer: {at: Time?, basis: Ref[], reason: text}?` make closure and deferral explicit. Refused new meanings use a new concern linked to the old one; old evidence is not rebound.

For verification, save `verification_of: {concern: Ref, cycle_id: UUID, studies: Ref[], scope: [{target: Ref, requirement_ids: text[]}]}?` on the attempt before delivery. Each study must be completed, refer to that concern and cover its named scope. Several completed explanations followed by one check form **one** cycle outcome for that concern, not one failure per explanation. Retrying verification of the same teaching uses the same cycle ID and adds evidence within that cycle; it cannot create a second unsuccessful cycle. A new cycle requires a newly completed teaching intervention. Within a concern, each teaching episode belongs to at most one cycle, and all attempts with that cycle ID retain the same teaching/scope binding. Prior teaching remains exposure but cannot be repackaged as another cycle's intervention. This binding describes earlier teaching and must not be placed in `Assistance.study`, which describes actual help during the response. Re-delivery/correction remains the same attempt observation. Derive at most one outcome per cycle from effective evidence: qualifying independent success resolves it; otherwise supported verification errors make it unsuccessful; exclusively missing/unjudgeable/assisted evidence leaves it incomplete. Preserve the underlying ordered observations and correction effects.

Diagnostic presentation uses the existing saved command boundary with a small reservation protocol. Before delivery, atomically reserve the declared request count for the exact attempt/session/concern. Retrying the same command/draft reuses its reservation. The browser then acknowledges actual presentation. The saved presentation has `{reservation_id, request_count, state: reserved/confirmed/not_presented/uncertain, reserved_at, presented_at?}` with the existing time-evidence quality. Reserved or uncertain allowance is unavailable to competing starts; it is not reported as confirmed learner burden. A failure known to occur before any delivery can release the reservation as not_presented. A crash after possible display cannot release it just because acknowledgement is absent. Reopening resumes the same draft/reservation and can confirm presentation; it does not authorize a second copy or reset the cap. Keep possible exposure distinct from confirmed presentation/reading. No transaction claims to make a browser paint atomic with a database write.

Resolve corrections, affected concern decisions and schedule projections in the same accepted revision transaction. Preserve actual historical effort and prompt presentations. A correction may invalidate a supposed failure cycle while retaining the fact that the learner spent time on it. Replay never calls the model, presents a prompt or adds a new exposure. Reservations from restored history remain part of the same allowance; timer restore rules do not erase them or grant a new presentation automatically.

This is a logical payload delta, not final language-specific types. The data audit must check reference roles, uncertain times and complete receipt membership for the added fields before implementation. There is no released format requiring fabricated migration fixtures today; once a version is released, later changes owe the existing migration guarantees.

## 8. Validation and stopping point

The [second cross-contract audit](cross-contract-audit.md) identified seven additional lifecycle/integration gaps. The [lifecycle patch](learning-lifecycle-contract.md), [tracking patch](../learning-observability/tracking-contract.md) and [closure traces](cross-contract-closure.md) now specify and reconcile all seven, including all fifteen counterexamples. This completes that requested design patch; policy calibration, implemented correctness, semantic quality and learning benefit are not established by these documents.

The [worked cases and acceptance pack](everyday-learning-cases.md) cover each rule with outcomes that would differ if it were ignored. They are design expectations, not passing runtime tests. The two focused research notes constrain [fresh checks and evidence age](research/fresh-checks-and-evidence-age.md) and [bounded repair and practice budgets](research/bounded-repair-and-practice-budget.md). Neither can supply the learner's preferred burden trade-off or prove these defaults efficient.

For implementation qualification, test real stored transitions, replay, exposure capture and browser navigation. Independently review fresh generated exercises and judgments across facts, language, mathematics and text-described procedures; inspect both correct and plausible alternative responses. Keep actual execution claims outside a text-only path. Measure full authoring/correction/practice burden, unnecessary diagnostics, unresolved concerns and genuinely delayed performance separately. Synthetic cases cannot supply those outcomes.

Design readiness requires all five areas to have a rule, a visible learner flow, saved evidence and an adversarial case; no conflicting cap or undocumented ranking version; and an explicit disposition for the defaults, now recorded in Design Baseline 1. Runtime correctness, semantic quality and learning benefit remain separate later gates. Future revisions should discuss a concrete policy consequence or observed failure; finalization requires no repeat scope approval or another demonstration exercise.
