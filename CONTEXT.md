# Memorization-oriented learning

Working vocabulary for a general learning system that helps learners retain knowledge across subjects, including facts, relationships, connected structures, and procedures.

## Language

**Memorization-oriented learning**: Learning whose central aim is to retain and retrieve knowledge over time, including its meaning and connections. It can include procedural practice when execution is part of the learner's goal.
_Avoid_: Rote recall only, Kubernetes training

**Knowledge component**: A meaningful fact, relation, principle, or procedure that can be assessed with an interpretable criterion. Smaller is useful only while the distinction changes diagnosis or practice.
_Avoid_: Infinitely atomic fact, card

**Relation**: A scoped claim connecting knowledge components, such as an explanation, contrast, applicability condition, or composition. A relation can itself be knowledge the learner aims to retain.

**Knowledge group**: A set of components and relationships organized by a shared principle, explanation, or procedure. The learner can retrieve the group as a larger structure while its parts remain meaningful.

**Capability**: The ability to perform a specified action on knowledge under specified conditions, such as explaining a relation or independently diagnosing a fault.
_Avoid_: A single universal mastery score

**Learning target**: A capability selected for the learner's current goal. Selecting a target does not require a separate exercise or memory estimate for every possible assessment mode.

**Assessment criterion**: A rule for interpreting evidence in a particular exercise. A criterion can help assess a capability without being a separate learning target.

**Exercise**: A question or task that gives the learner an opportunity to practice and demonstrate one or more capabilities.
_Avoid_: Knowledge component

**Exercise family**: Related tasks with stated shared capability demands and allowed variations. Belonging to one family does not establish equal difficulty or identical evidence.

**Fresh instance**: A task that requires applying the intended capability to an instance whose answer cannot be supplied merely by copying the earlier solution. Freshness is relative to the target and prior exposure; repeated cues remain appropriate for direct fact recall.

**Learning concern**: A continuing, evidence-linked reason to inspect an answer or provide help, with a known scope or explicit uncertainty. A concern is not automatically a diagnosed knowledge gap.

**Repair cycle**: Completed focused teaching followed by a check of the same scoped requirement. Missing or unjudgeable verification leaves the cycle incomplete; it is not evidence of unsuccessful learning.

**Practice bundle**: Learning targets selected to be addressed together in one exercise. Their grouping is a practice choice and can change without changing the knowledge they concern.
_Avoid_: Knowledge group

**Planned coverage**: The capabilities an exercise is intended to elicit. Planning or selecting the exercise does not establish that the learner demonstrated them.

**Observed coverage**: The capabilities supported by the actual response under its assessment conditions and assistance. It can be narrower than planned coverage.

**Session budget**: The time available for a practice session, including preparation, answering, feedback and follow-up work. Reaching the budget does not resolve remaining knowledge uncertainty.

**Assessment mode**: The kind of performance requested, such as recognition, unaided recall, explanation, or execution.
_Avoid_: Interchangeable question format

**Attempt**: One learner encounter with a particular exercise, including help used, actions, response, and outcome.

**Observation**: Evidence about a specified capability from an attempt, with its scope and conditions. A correct task outcome may support fewer claims than the task's prerequisite list.
_Avoid_: Proof of mastery

**Learner estimate**: An inference about what a learner may remember or understand, based on available observations and stated assumptions. It can concern knowledge that was not directly tested.
_Avoid_: Observed success

**Uncertainty**: The limits of confidence in a learner estimate. Weak evidence about a capability is different from evidence that the capability is weak.

**Diagnostic exercise**: An exercise selected to distinguish possible gaps in the learner's knowledge. Its value as a diagnosis is separate from how much practice it provides.

**Prerequisite**: Knowledge or ability that the curriculum proposes is needed for another capability. A prerequisite relationship does not by itself show that practice occurred.
_Avoid_: Reinforcement edge

**Relational capability**: The ability to explain, derive, distinguish, or apply a relationship between knowledge components.
_Avoid_: Merely remembering that two topics are linked

**Transfer**: A change in performance on a different task, context, or assessment mode following practice. Transfer must be assessed; related topics alone do not establish it.

**Fluency**: Reliable, sufficiently timely, independent performance across relevant contexts. Fast typing alone is not fluency.

**Review compression**: Reducing separate review work when one exercise provides sufficient evidence or practice for several targets.
_Avoid_: Automatic credit for every ancestor

**Adaptive chunking**: Changing the size of a review from individual components to connected groups as familiarity develops, and returning to detail when a gap appears.
_Avoid_: Deleting the underlying knowledge components

**Delayed check**: An assessment after a meaningful interval, preferably using an unseen variant, that distinguishes immediate performance from retained capability.

**Measurement cohort**: A named, fixed set of learning targets and endpoint rules used to compare evidence under stated conditions. Changing its membership creates a different comparison.

**Endpoint**: A designated opportunity to assess a target at a stated horizon and under stated conditions. A missing or unresolved endpoint is not a failed answer.

**Observed improvement**: A change from supported error to independent success on comparable checks. It does not by itself establish that the learning system caused the change.

**Maintenance**: Previously demonstrated capability remaining successful at a later opportunity, with intervening practice and effort visible. Maintenance can be useful without adding new capabilities.

**Exposure**: A recorded opportunity to encounter or retrieve target-relevant content, including practice, help or an answer-bearing inspection. Presentation alone does not prove attention, study completion or learning.

**Full learning effort**: The effort associated with achieving or maintaining a learning goal, including preparation, practice, diagnosis, feedback, correction, progress inspection and blocking waits. Shared work counts once; unknown effort remains unknown.

**Archived curriculum**: A curriculum the learner has stopped actively studying while preserving its material and learning history. Archiving does not erase evidence or end work on shared targets in other active curricula.

**Saved learning report**: A retained account of learning evidence and effort for a stated scope and evaluation time. Later corrections can change a new report without silently rewriting what the older report said.

**Removed learning evidence**: Evidence intentionally made unavailable through deletion. Its absence does not mean the learner answered incorrectly, skipped a check or lost the knowledge.
